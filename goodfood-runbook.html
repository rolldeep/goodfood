Good Food Runbook

A repeatable way to find what is hurting your eating and fix it one habit at a time. Answer the triage questions, run the top plays, and review every Sunday. It borrows from Bryan Johnson's Blueprint, Michael Pollan, Peter Attia, BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits, and published nutrition research.

      Step 3

      Weekly review and escalation

      Sunday review, 15 minutes

      1. Retake the triage above with this week in mind.
      2. Check the tracking number for each play you ran. Did it move?
      3. For each play, decide to keep it (working, not yet automatic), graduate it (automatic now), or shrink it (too hard, make the step smaller).
      4. Pick no more than one new play to add.
      5. Write the shopping list and put one 60-minute prep block in your calendar.

      If you missed a day, don't treat it as failure. Shrink the habit until you can't skip it. BJ Fogg's rule is that a habit should be so easy you can do it on your worst day.

      Escalate to a professional when

      • You binge (eating a lot while feeling out of control), purge, or make up for eating with fasting or exercise. Contact your doctor or an eating-disorder helpline. Skip calorie tracking and fasting plays.
      • You take diabetes or blood-pressure medication. Changing meal timing or carbs can shift your doses, so check with your doctor first.
      • You are pregnant or breastfeeding, have kidney disease, or have a diagnosed GI condition. A registered dietitian should set your protein, fiber and fasting targets.
      • You lose or gain weight quickly without explanation, or you feel tired or dizzy all the time.

      Bryan Johnson's full Blueprint protocol, with its strict calorie limits, supplements and very early last meal, is an extreme personal experiment run with medical supervision. This runbook uses its principles, not its protocol.

      Sources

      • Bryan Johnson, Blueprint protocol and Don't Die: no added sugar, no alcohol, mostly whole plants, early last meal to protect sleep, measure everything.
      • Michael Pollan, In Defense of Food (2008) and Food Rules (2009): "Eat food. Not too much. Mostly plants."
      • Peter Attia, Outlive (2023): protein targets well above the 0.8 g/kg RDA.
      • BJ Fogg, Tiny Habits (2019): anchor, tiny behavior, celebrate.
      • Hall et al., Cell Metabolism (2019): people on an ultra-processed diet ate about 500 kcal/day more than on an unprocessed diet.
      • McDonald et al., American Gut Project, mSystems (2018): eating 30+ plant types a week was linked to a more diverse gut microbiome. Tim Spector (ZOE) popularized the target.
      • WHO guideline on sugars (2015): free sugars under 10% of energy, ideally under 5%. WHO statement (2023): no safe level of alcohol for health.
